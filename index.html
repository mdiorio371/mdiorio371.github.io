Selected work

Structural variation in bacterial genomes

PhD research · Ken Dewar Lab, McGill University

Built a documented, modular pipeline that processes thousands of complete bacterial genomes, detects structural variants across samples, and integrates that variation into phylogenetic and population inference from SNP-based typing. Two thesis chapters are R packages designed for structured, shareable, end-to-end analysis.

Human RNA-seq analysis, joint capsule tissue

Lead computational analyst · manuscript in preparation

Owned the full computational analysis end to end, from raw sequencing files through to the figures and statistics in the manuscript. Built the pipeline from scratch (HISAT2, featureCounts, DESeq2), set documented QC thresholds, and packaged everything into reproducible notebooks that regenerate every result. One case group had only two samples, so I designed three complementary analyses and reported the robust intersection rather than lean on a single fragile model.
